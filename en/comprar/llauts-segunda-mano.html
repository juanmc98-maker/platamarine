Used llaüts (traditional Mediterranean boats)

By Juan MoranteUpdated October 2026

The llaüt is the classic boat of the western Mediterranean: handsome, seaworthy and with a relaxed pace that gets you hooked. Many are bought and sold between acquaintances, which is why they are hard to find. If you are looking for one, here is what I check and how I can help.

What a llaüt is

It is a traditional boat from the Catalan, Valencian and Balearic coasts, with a high bow and stern, a long keel and, today, a slow-revving inboard diesel. It used to be a lateen-rigged fishing boat; now most are used for cruising, anchoring in coves and relaxed fishing. In Spanish it is called laúd.

  • Wooden: the traditional ones, many built by local shipwrights. Beautiful and very seaworthy, but they need maintenance every year.
  • GRP: modern boats with the llaüt's lines and far less maintenance.

What to check on a wooden llaüt

  • Caulking and planking: seams between planks, weeping areas and soft planks.
  • Keel, stem and sternpost: wood rots first where fresh water collects.
  • Bilge: how much water comes in while moored. Some is normal on wood; a lot is not.
  • Engine and shaft: hours, stern tube and stuffing box.
  • History: when it was last hauled out and what was done.

On a wooden llaüt I would always get a survey, ideally by someone who knows wood. The general checklist is in what to check when buying a used boat.

Licence and papers

The licence depends on the length on the papers, as with any boat: Licencia de Navegación up to 6 metres, PNB up to 8 and PER above that. Many llaüts are between 6 and 9 metres. Check the ITB, that there are no charges and that the paperwork is up to date. See boating licences and papers and change of ownership.

How much it costs

It varies enormously. A wooden llaüt that needs work can be cheap to buy and expensive afterwards; a restored one with invoices is worth much more. So rather than the asking price, I look at the condition and what will need investing in the first year. For a general reference, see how much a boat costs.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between llaüt, llagut and laúd?

None: llaüt and llagut are two Catalan spellings, and laúd is the Spanish word.

Is a wooden llaüt expensive to maintain?

More than a GRP one: a yearly haul-out with caulking and paint is almost a must. If you want fewer worries, a GRP llaüt is easier.

Where can I find a used llaüt?

Mainly in the Balearics and along the Catalan and Valencian coast, and many are sold between acquaintances or in the port itself. If you create an alert, I will let you know when one comes in.
