Which kind of motorboat suits you

  • Open and RIB: for day trips, swimming and fishing. The simplest to maintain.
  • Sundeck: large sunpad and a small cabin to sleep aboard now and then.
  • Walkaround: you can walk around the cabin; a good compromise between fishing and family.
  • Pilothouse: enclosed helm, for use all year round.
  • Cabin cruiser: for spending days aboard.

I explain them with photos in the guide to types of motorboat, and if in doubt, the two-minute quiz will help.

Outboard or inboard

A petrol outboard is easier and cheaper to maintain and can be replaced without touching the boat. An inboard or sterndrive, especially diesel, uses less fuel and gives more range on larger boats, but servicing costs more. I compare them in detail in the article on propulsion systems.

What they cost

As a guide, on the Catalan coast: a 6 to 7 metre open boat from roughly €20,000 to €60,000, a 7 to 8.5 metre sundeck or walkaround from €25,000 to over €100,000, and an 8 metre pilothouse from €45,000 to €110,000. Ranges by model and year are in used boat prices.

Frequently asked questions

Which motorboat should I buy to start with?

To start, a 6 to 7 metre open or sundeck with an outboard: easy to handle, to maintain and to sell on later. You can skipper it with the PNB.

Is an outboard or an inboard better?

For boats up to 8 metres used in summer, an outboard usually makes more sense. On larger boats, or if you go out a lot, a diesel inboard uses less fuel.

How many engine hours is a lot on a used boat?

It depends more on the engine and how it has been maintained than on the figure: a high-hours engine with documented servicing can be in better shape than a low-hours one left idle for years.
