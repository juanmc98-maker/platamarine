Selling your boat with its berth: how the transfer works

By Juan MoranteUpdated October 20266 min read

One of the first things any buyer asks is whether the boat comes with a berth. The answer is rarely a simple yes or no: it depends on the type of berth you have and on your marina's rules. Here are the cases, what can be transferred and what to be clear about before you advertise.

You don't own a berth like a flat

In Spain, ports are public domain. Even when people talk about "buying a berth", what is bought is a right to use it for a period, usually until the marina's concession ends. That right expires and is subject to the rules of the marina and of the authority that oversees it.

So before promising anything to a buyer, you need to know exactly what kind of berth you have. The guide to berths in Catalonia covers the types of port and prices.

If your berth is rented

This is the most common case: you pay an annual, seasonal or monthly fee to the marina or club. The contract is yours, not the boat's, so in general it does not pass with the sale: the buyer will have to apply for their own berth, and some marinas have waiting lists.

What you can do is ask the marina office whether they would accept the buyer in your berth when you leave it. If they say yes, get it in writing. If not, say in the listing that the berth is rented and not included.

If you hold a right of use

If you once paid for the right to use a berth (what many people call an "owned berth"), it can usually be transferred, but almost never freely. Depending on your title and the marina's rules, you will usually need to:

  • Notify or obtain authorisation from the company running the marina or the port authority.
  • Be up to date with fees, utilities and maintenance charges.
  • Pay any transfer fees set by the marina.
  • Put it in writing, and in some cases before a notary or in the Registry.

Also check when the concession ends: the buyer only acquires the remaining years, which has a big impact on value.

If it is a yacht club berth

At many clubs the berth is tied to membership: it is not sold with the boat, and the buyer would have to join and be allocated a berth under the club's rules. Some clubs allow a change of holder in certain cases; others do not. The only way to know is to ask the club secretary and get the answer in writing.

Papers to get before you sell

  • The berth contract or title (rental or right of use) and the concession end date.
  • A statement from the marina confirming there are no outstanding debts.
  • The marina's or club's rules or transfer conditions.
  • The berth's maximum dimensions (length and beam), in case the buyer wants to use it for another boat.
  • The current fees (maintenance, water, electricity, storage), so the buyer knows what they will pay.

How to put it in the listing and the contract

A clear sentence is better than "with berth" that later cannot be delivered. For example: "rented berth at the marina, not included" or "right of use until 2035, transferable with the marina's authorisation, sold separately".

If the berth is sold together with the boat, the contract should show separate prices for the boat and the berth, because they are two different things, each with its own formalities and tax treatment. And if the sale depends on the marina authorising the transfer, make it a written condition, including in the deposit (arras) document.

Frequently asked questions

Can I sell my boat with the berth included?

If you hold a right of use, usually yes, following your marina's transfer conditions. If the berth is rented or a club berth, it generally does not pass with the boat: the buyer has to apply for their own.

Is an "owned" berth mine forever?

No. Ports are public domain and rights of use last at most until the concession ends. Check the date on your title.

Who can tell me if it can be transferred?

The marina office or the club secretary, depending on who manages your berth. Always get the answer in writing before advertising.

Is the berth taxed the same as the boat?

Not necessarily: they are separate transactions and the tax treatment can vary by case and region. Check with your tax adviser before agreeing the price.
