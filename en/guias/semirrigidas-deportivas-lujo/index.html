How to choose a luxury performance RIB from 10 to 13 metres

By Juan MoranteUpdated October 20266 min read

People looking for a fast boat to take out into open water often end up looking at large RIBs. This guide explains how they differ from a standard RIB or a speedboat, what to compare between models and what one really costs once registered in Spain.

What a luxury performance RIB is

A RIB has a rigid hull with inflatable tubes all around it. The tubes add buoyancy and stability, and protect the boat when berthing.

In the luxury segment, from about 10 metres, you also get a hull designed to run fast in a seaway (deep V, often stepped), two or more high-power outboards, a console with a toilet or even a bed, and a finish that has nothing to do with a basic tender. Many owners use it as a day boat or as a yacht tender.

Luxury RIB, standard RIB or speedboat

Compared with a standard RIB, the main differences are the hull, the power and the design category. A 6 to 8 metre RIB is usually built for coastal cruising; a 12 metre category B RIB, for open water.

Compared with a speedboat of the same length, the tubes add stability but also take up beam, so compare the real usable space. A speedboat usually has more cabin. If you want to spend several days on board, a cabin cruiser will probably suit you better; if you want to go out, run fast and anchor in coves, a large RIB is the better fit.

What to compare between models

  • Length and inner beam. Overall length is misleading on a RIB because the tubes take up space. Compare the inner beam, which sets the real deck space.
  • Hull. The deadrise angle (a deep V usually cuts through the waves better) and whether it is stepped, which reduces drag at high speed.
  • Design category. Category B is meant for open water (winds up to force 8 and significant wave height up to 4 metres); C is for coastal waters. Being certified for those conditions does not mean you can go fast in them.
  • Protection. T-top, hardtop or glass windscreen. At 50 knots it makes a big difference.
  • Console. Whether it has a toilet, a bed or just a locker. It changes the kind of day you can have on board.
  • Engines. How many, the maximum power the hull accepts and which brands the yard offers. The engine package moves the price by tens of thousands of euros.
  • Authorised capacity. The maximum number of people on board is set by the boat’s documents when it is registered in Spain. If the yard certifies more than 12, check before buying.
  • Licence. Up to 15 metres in length, the Spanish PER is enough. To cross from the mainland to the Balearic Islands you also need the corresponding endorsement. See licences.

What it really costs in Spain

Many yards publish ex-works prices without taxes. On top of the price you need to add:

  • 21 % VAT.
  • Registration tax (IEDMT). Over 8 metres, the first registration in Spain for private use pays 12 % on the price before VAT. It depends on the use (charter may be exempt) and on the flag. I explain it in the registration tax guide.
  • Transport from the yard, registration and commissioning.
  • Factory options you choose: carbon, teak, electronics, upholstery…

Worked example

Indicative example for a first registration in Spain for private use. A SKIPPER ACE 360 with 2 Mercury Verado 300 V8 starts from €329,000 ex works. With VAT (€69,090) and IEDMT at 12 % for private use (€39,480), it comes to about €437,570 before transport, registration and options. These figures are indicative: the exact amount is in the official quote, and the tax should be confirmed with a registration agent.

Then come the running costs: berth, engine servicing, insurance and haul-out. I go through them in how much it costs to keep a boat in Catalonia.

When a SKIPPER fits and when it doesn’t

It fits if you want a very fast day boat for open water, if you cruise between the mainland and the Balearics, or if you are looking for a tender worthy of a large yacht.

It doesn’t fit if you want to sleep on board for several days in a row (a cabin cruiser suits you better), if you will mostly cruise near the harbour at low speed, or if your budget is well below €400,000 on the water. In that case, a used RIB or a smaller model can give you 80 % of the experience for much less.

If you are torn between models, the most reliable way to decide is to try one. There is no demo boat in Spain yet, so sea trials are arranged at the yard.
