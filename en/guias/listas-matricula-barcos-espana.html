The 9 Spanish boat registration lists, explained

By Juan MoranteUpdated October 20266 min read

Every boat registered in Spain is entered on a “list”, and that number is the first thing in its registration. If you buy or sell a recreational boat you will nearly always see list 6 or 7, but the second-hand market also has boats that come from fishing or from a public body. Here are all nine, what each one means and which affect you.

What a list is

The Ship Registry (Registro de Matrícula de Buques) is kept by each Harbour Master’s Office (Capitanía Marítima) and is organised in books called “lists”. Each boat is entered on the one that matches its origin and activity. This is set out in article 4 of Royal Decree 1027/1989 on ship flagging and registration. For recreational boats from 2.5 to 24 metres, Royal Decree 1435/2010 also applies.

How to read a registration number

The registration (“indicativo de matrícula”) combines, in this order, the list, the maritime province, the maritime district and the folio with the year of registration. For example, a registration like 7ª-BA-2-123-18 would read as list 7, maritime province of Barcelona, district 2, folio 123, registered in 2018 (an invented example to show the format).

On recreational boats it must appear on both bows, as high as possible above the waterline, large enough to read and in a colour that contrasts with the hull. Tenders carry the main boat’s registration with “Aux” in brackets.

Lists 6 and 7: the ones that matter for leisure boats

The difference between them is profit. List 6 is for recreational boats run commercially (rental, charter); list 7 is for private use: cruising, sport or non-professional fishing. Registration tax, inspections and insurance all change; it’s covered in detail in the guide to list 6 and list 7, and if you’re thinking of renting out your boat, in Can I charter out my boat to cover the costs?.

CE-marked recreational boats up to 12 metres also have a simplified registration regime (article 8 of RD 1435/2010). If that’s your case, ask the Harbour Master’s Office to confirm it.

Lists 3 and 4: boats that come from fishing

Every now and then boats registered on list 3 (professional fishing) or list 4 (fishing and aquaculture auxiliaries) come up for sale. Many traditional llaüts and wooden boats have that origin. To use one as a leisure boat, the change to list 7 has to be processed at the Harbour Master’s Office, which may involve an inspection, paperwork and upgrading the safety equipment.

Before buying one, ask which list it is on today, whether the change has already been made and what is missing. Don’t assume you can use it like any other leisure boat while it is still on a fishing list.

List 9 and the complementary list

List 9 is a provisional register: boats under construction are entered there from the moment the build is authorised, except series-built sports boats. When the boat is finished, that entry is cancelled and the permanent one is opened on the appropriate list.

Separately, boats imported under provisional flag are entered on a special complementary list of the list that applies to them (article 4.2 of RD 1027/1989).

What to check when buying

  1. The list shown today in the documents, and that it matches the registration on the hull.
  2. If it comes from list 6, the date it switched to commercial use and whether any registration tax is outstanding.
  3. If it comes from list 3 or 4, whether the change to list 7 has been completed.
  4. If it is a list 8 boat (public body), how it was deregistered and which documents transfer it.
