Mandatory safety equipment on board in Spain: what to carry in each zone

By Juan MoranteUpdated October 20266 min read

Buying a boat and then finding out it is short of flares, the extinguisher has not been serviced or the life jackets are not right for its zone happens more often than you would think. Here is what the current Spanish rules, Royal Decree 339/2021, require, sorted by navigation zone so you can check it before you buy or before you head out.

The 7 navigation zones

Spanish rules divide the sea into seven zones depending on how far you go from the coast or from shelter. The lower the number, the further you can go and the more equipment you need.

Zone 7Sheltered waters, ports and bays
Zone 6Up to 2 miles from shelter
Zone 5Up to 5 miles from shelter
Zone 4Up to 12 miles offshore
Zone 3Up to 25 miles
Zone 2Up to 60 miles
Zone 1Unlimited

Your equipment has to cover the furthest zone you will sail in. If you are not sure which zones your boat may go to, check its papers or ask the Harbour Master.

Life jackets

You need at least one life jacket per person on board, plus one extra in zone 1. Children must have one that fits their size and weight. Minimum buoyancy depends on the zone:

  • 100 N in zones 5, 6 and 7.
  • 150 N in zones 2, 3 and 4.
  • 275 N in zone 1.

Each life jacket has its own light. In zones 4 to 7, if you only sail by day, the rules allow you to do without it.

Inflatable life jackets are serviced as the manufacturer specifies, at an authorised service station. If you are buying a boat, check the last service label on each one.

Flares, rockets and smoke signals

  • Zones 5 and 6: 3 hand flares.
  • Zone 4: 3 hand flares and 3 parachute rockets.
  • Zones 2 and 3: 6 flares, 6 rockets and 1 floating smoke signal.
  • Zone 1: 6 flares, 6 rockets and 2 smoke signals.

They are not required in zone 7. Every pack carries an expiry date: check it and replace any that are about to expire. Expired flares do not go in the bin; ask your marina or chandlery where to hand them in.

Fire extinguishers

If the boat has a CE mark, the extinguishers are the ones the builder lists in the owner's manual. If it does not, the rules take into account length and engine power:

  • By length: 1 type 34B extinguisher up to 15 m (under 10 m, only if there is an enclosed living space), 2 from 15 to 20 m and 3 from 20 to 24 m.
  • By engine power: 1 type 34B extinguisher up to 220 kW (not required for outboards up to 25 kW) and, above 220 kW, a total capacity of 0.3 × the power.

Each extinguisher must hold at least 2 kg of agent, and one must be within reach of the helm. When buying, check the last service label and that the seal is intact.

First aid kit

In zones 1 to 4, boats without professional crew must carry a first aid kit with the contents of the “life raft” type (annex II of Royal Decree 258/1999), together with the medical guide. In zones 5 to 7 it is not compulsory, but a basic kit is always a good idea. When buying a boat, check the dates: out-of-date medicines are very common.

Bilge pumping and anchoring

  • Zone 7: a manual or electric pump.
  • Zones 4, 5 and 6: a manual or electric pump and a bucket of at least 5 litres.
  • Zones 1, 2 and 3: a pump driven by the engine or another power source, a manual pump and two buckets of at least 5 litres.

The anchor line must be at least five times the boat's length, with a length of chain equal to the boat's length. Boats of 6 metres or less may use rope only. The anchor weight depends on the boat's length.

VHF radio and EPIRB

Royal Decree 339/2021 does not cover radio or EPIRBs: they have their own regulations. Even if your zone does not require one, a VHF listening on channel 16 is one of the most useful things to have if something goes wrong at sea.

What to check before buying a boat

  • The boat's papers: which zones it may sail in and how many people it can carry.
  • The life jackets: enough for everyone, with the buoyancy for that zone and, if inflatable, serviced.
  • The flares and rockets: the expiry date on each pack.
  • The extinguishers: service label and seal.
  • The last ITB inspection: if defects were recorded, ask for them to be fixed before the sale.

Missing or expired items need not stop the purchase. They are one more point to discuss on price.

Source

  • Royal Decree 339/2021 of 18 May on safety and pollution-prevention equipment for leisure craft (consolidated text, BOE, in Spanish). It replaced Order FOM/1144/2003 and was amended by Royal Decree 587/2022 regarding life rafts.
