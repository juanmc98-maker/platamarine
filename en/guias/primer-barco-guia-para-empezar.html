Buying your first boat without getting lost along the way

By Juan MoranteSeptember 20269 min read

If this is the first time you are thinking about buying a boat, it is normal for the part you do not understand to hold you back more than the part that excites you: what paperwork is needed, whether the engine in the listing is in good shape, how much it will cost to keep once you have it. This guide is about exactly that: getting you to the starting point before you look at listings, so that when the time comes you know what to ask and what to check.

Before looking at listings, work out which boat you need

The most common mistake is not buying a bad boat, it is buying the wrong one: too big for how you will really use it, designed for long passages when you will actually go out for four hours at weekends, or the other way round. First of all, be clear about what you will use it for (short trips, fishing, family, the odd crossing), how many people usually come aboard and where you will keep it moored, because that last point also shapes the budget, as you will see below.

If you want a quick way to narrow it down, we have a short test that points you towards the type of boat and the length that suit you: take the "Which boat do I need?" test.

The paperwork you will come across

This is where most questions come up, and rightly so: buying a used boat in Spain has its own procedures, different from those for a car. The points worth understanding before you sign anything:

  • Registration list (List 6 or List 7): it determines whether the boat can be chartered or is for private use only, and it affects taxes and inspections. I explain it in the guide to List 6 and List 7.
  • IEDMT (the "registration tax"): when it is paid, when there is an exemption and why so many boats measure 7.99 metres. You have it in the registration tax guide.
  • VAT: in a sale between private individuals it is not always clear whether the price includes it or not. It is one of the questions you should always ask the seller, never take it for granted.
  • ITB (the technical inspection): like the MOT for a car, but for boats. Which boats need it and how often is explained in the ITB guide.

None of these procedures is a reason to rule out a boat you like: they are things you check before buying, not problems that appear afterwards. If you prefer to have it all in one place, there is a summary of paperwork and taxes bringing all these guides together.

What to check on the boat, beyond the photos

A nice listing tells you nothing about the real condition of the engine, or whether the hull has had a repaired knock. Before deciding on a used boat there is a list of very specific things worth checking: engine and hours, electronics, hull, up-to-date paperwork. I set it out in detail in what to check when buying a used boat.

How much it costs to keep a boat each year

This is the question almost nobody asks before buying and the one that causes the most regret afterwards. The purchase price is only part of it; the rest is the cost of owning it:

  • Mooring: the biggest fixed cost, and it varies a lot with the port and the length. You have real prices for 19 ports in Catalonia in the mooring guide.
  • Insurance: at least third-party liability is compulsory.
  • Engine maintenance: regular services, more so for a diesel with many hours.
  • Haul-out and winter storage: taking it out of the water to clean the hull and check it over, usually once or twice a year.
  • ITB: every so often, depending on the type of boat, as explained in its guide.

There is no single figure that works for every boat, as it depends a lot on the length, the engine and where you keep it, but it is a calculation worth doing calmly before buying, not after signing.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to be a boating expert to buy a boat?

No. Most people buying their first boat are not. What matters is not to rush and to check the basics before deciding: paperwork, engine and what you will really use it for.

Is it cheaper to buy a new boat or a used one?

A used boat usually works out better value if you do not mind it having some hours of use, but it needs a good check first: the initial saving can disappear if the engine has been worked hard.

What paperwork should the seller of a used boat give me?

At a minimum: the registration record or certificate of seaworthiness, the last ITB certificate if applicable, and proof that the boat has no outstanding charges against it. If any of this is missing, it is a reason to ask before going any further.

Can I finance a used boat?

Yes, several lenders allow it, although the terms vary with the age and type of boat.

This guide is for guidance only and does not replace advice on your specific case: the taxes, paperwork and timescales for each boat depend on its list, its length and its history.
