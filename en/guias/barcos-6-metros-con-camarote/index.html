6-metre boats with a cabin: what to expect and what to check before you buy

By Juan MoranteUpdated October 20265 min read

A boat of around 6 metres with a cabin is the most common way into boating: it fits a small berth, it can go on a trailer and it has somewhere to shelter or have a nap. But it isn’t a cruiser. Here’s what it can do, and what to check before you buy one.

What a cabin gives you in 6 metres

  • A shelter, not a bedroom. Usually there’s a berth in the bow for two people, with little headroom. It’s for keeping things dry, getting changed, getting out of the sun or spending the odd summer night at anchor.
  • The toilet, if there is one, is basic. On many boats it’s a portable or chemical toilet under the berth or tucked into a space. If a toilet matters to you, ask before you go and see the boat.
  • What you gain in the cabin you lose in the cockpit. A 6 m boat with a cabin has less room outside than an open boat of the same length. Think about how many of you usually go out and where you’ll spend most of the day.

Three ways to fit a cabin

TypeWhat it’s likeWho it suits
Cuddy cabin or walkaroundCabin in the bow and side decks to walk to the frontFishing, anchoring and day trips
PilothouseThe helm is enclosed, with the cabin below or in frontPeople who also go out outside summer or when it’s windy
Day cruiser or sundeckSunpad on the bow and a small cabin underneathSwimming, coves and time in the sun

The exact length matters more than it seems

What counts is the hull length in the boat’s papers, not the model name. And around 6 metres there are two thresholds in Spain:

  • Licence. With the Licencia de Navegación you can skipper boats up to 6 metres with the engine power the builder allows, in daylight only and no more than 2 miles from a port or sheltered spot. Over 6 m, you need the PNB (up to 8 m and 5 miles) or a higher licence. See licences.
  • ITB (technical inspection). On list 7 (private use), boats under 6 metres have no periodic inspections; from 6 metres up, at most every 5 years. I explain it in the ITB guide.

Under 8 metres there’s no registration tax, so at this length it isn’t a factor. If you want to know why, it’s in the guide to the registration tax (IEDMT).

What to check before buying a used one

  • Damp in the cabin: a musty smell, stains on the headlining or the cushions, and where the water gets in (hatch, portholes, deck fittings).
  • Engine: year, hours and invoices, and a cold start. Almost all have an outboard; if it’s an inboard or a sterndrive, having a mechanic check it matters even more.
  • Hull and transom: no cracks or damp around the engine.
  • Paperwork: registry sheet (hoja de asiento) with no charges and a clear hull length.
  • Trailer, if it comes with one: papers, ITV (roadworthiness test) if it’s over 750 kg, and check that your car and your driving licence can tow it.

What it costs afterwards

A berth based on length (or storage and a trailer), compulsory third-party insurance, yearly engine servicing and antifouling if it stays in the water. I put numbers on it in the guide to what it costs to keep a boat in Catalonia. And if you buy from a private seller, there’s ITP (transfer tax): 5% in Catalonia (each region’s rate is in the ITP guide).
