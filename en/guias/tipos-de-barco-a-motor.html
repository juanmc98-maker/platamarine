Open, sundeck, pilothouse, cabin cruiser, flybridge: which motor boat fits how you'll use it

By Juan MoranteUpdated September 20268 min read

Before looking at specific models, it helps to be clear on which family of boat you actually need. Heading out for a swim with friends on Saturdays isn't the same as sleeping aboard with the family. Here I explain the categories most commonly seen in Catalonia, what each one is for, and the typical lengths.

Open and centre console

An open deck, with no enclosed cabin or just a small forward shelter. Built for a day at the beach, swimming, light fishing and short trips. Easy to handle, good visibility from the centre console, simple maintenance. Typical lengths 5 to 8 metres, almost always with an outboard. This is the best-selling family on the Catalan coast, with models like the Jeanneau Cap Camarat or the Quicksilver Activ.

Sundeck and bowrider

Similar to the open but with more sunbathing area, bow-seating like a small cockpit, and a bit more comfort for spending the day aboard. Often come with a small head or forward cabin. Lengths of 6 to 9 metres. A good choice for families who want comfort without going all the way to a cabin cruiser.

Walkaround

A perimeter walkway that lets you move all the way around the deck, much valued for fishing and for handling anchoring or mooring safely. Combines well a cabin with a berth and a fishing or swimming area. Lengths of 7 to 10 metres. The Jeanneau Merry Fisher is the best-known reference in this category in the area.

Pilothouse (fishing-cruising)

An enclosed, sheltered helm station, built for handling bad weather or the off-season without getting cold. Combines fishing, cruising, and some range for a few days. Lengths of 8 to 12 metres. It's a smaller-volume category than the open, but with buyers very loyal to a particular brand and model.

Cabin cruiser

An enclosed cabin with a double berth, a galley and a head, built for sleeping aboard and short multi-day trips. This is where the "real" boat begins for many families ready to move up from a day at the beach to a week's holiday on the water. Lengths of 8 to 12 metres, with inboard engines from around 9-10 metres in most models. The Beneteau Antares and the Bavaria cabin cruiser are common at this size.

Flybridge

A second, raised helm station, with more social space on the upper deck. It adds comfort and visibility, but also more weight, more fuel burn and more maintenance. Starts from around 10-11 metres and is common in the premium segment: Sea Ray, Sessa, or the higher-end Beneteau (Gran Turismo) and Jeanneau (Leader) models fit one from a certain length up.

A category apart: RIBs

Not a family of rigid boat as such, but they share a market with the small open. Light, very manageable, ideal as a second boat or for anyone who prioritises simplicity over comfort. Highfield is a common brand in the area. Almost always with an outboard, lengths of 4 to 9 metres.

Outboard or inboard, in short

Outboards dominate up to about 9-10 metres: lighter, easier to inspect and replace, and with a good network of workshops in Catalonia (Yamaha, Suzuki, Mercury). Inboards matter more on larger cabin cruisers and flybridges, where they give better range and handle rough seas better, with Volvo Penta and Yanmar as the most common engines in the area.

Example with numbers

A family of four who want to go for lunch in a cove on weekends and spend the occasional night at anchor in summer doesn't need a 13-metre flybridge: a walkaround or a 9-10 metre cabin cruiser with a small outboard or inboard covers that use case at a much more reasonable budget and maintenance load. Moving from a 7-metre sundeck to a similarly aged 9.5-metre cabin cruiser typically means a difference in the order of €20,000-30,000, and in exchange you gain a double berth, an enclosed head and range for two nights: exactly what's missing for anyone who's outgrown a day boat.

My advice: liquidity and the 8-metre threshold

When I help someone choose, I don't just look at what boat they like today, I also look at how easy it'll be to sell the day they want to move on. Boats of 7 to 9 metres with an outboard are, by a good margin, the most liquid in the Catalan market: there's plenty of demand, plenty of comparable supply, and the buying and selling process is simpler. Under 8 metres, the boat also avoids the registration tax when bought or imported, which helps resale too; I cover it in the guide to the IEDMT and the 8-metre threshold. From 10-12 metres up, the market narrows, buyers are more specific, and time to sell tends to stretch out. That's not a reason to avoid a bigger boat if it's what you need, but it's worth factoring in if resale matters to you.

The lengths and brands in this guide are a general reference, based on what I typically see in the Catalan market; every manufacturer has models that cross these categories. Before deciding, try the boat on the water: how it handles matters as much as the spec sheet.
