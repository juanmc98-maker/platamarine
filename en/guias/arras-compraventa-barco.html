Deposits (arras) when buying a boat in Spain: types, how much and what to put in writing

By Juan MoranteUpdated October 20266 min read

When buyer and seller agree on a price but the boat still needs checking or the paperwork is not ready, the usual step is to pay a deposit. In Spain that deposit is called arras, and what happens to the money if someone pulls out depends on how it is worded. Here are the types, how much is usually paid and what to write down.

What arras are

Arras are a sum of money the buyer pays the seller to secure the deal while the final sale is prepared. With a boat this usually happens between the offer being accepted and the day the contract is signed, the balance is paid and the change of ownership is filed.

They do two things: the seller stops showing the boat to others, and the buyer makes sure it is not sold to someone else while they check it and arrange payment. What matters is what happens to the money if the deal falls through, and that depends on the type of arras.

The three types and what happens in each

  • Confirmatory. A deposit and part-payment of the price. They confirm there is a contract: if someone fails to perform, the other party can demand performance or claim damages, but nobody can simply walk away by losing the deposit. If the document says nothing, Spanish courts usually treat arras as this type.
  • Penitential. They allow either side to pull out at a price: a buyer who withdraws loses the deposit and a seller who withdraws pays back double. This is what article 1454 of the Spanish Civil Code provides, but it has to be clearly stated in writing to apply.
  • Penalty. They work as a penalty for breach: whoever breaches loses the deposit (or returns it doubled), but the other party can also demand performance of the contract. They are less common and must also be expressly agreed.

In Catalonia, the Catalan Civil Code regulates arras mainly with property sales in mind. For a boat, the sensible approach is the same everywhere: state clearly what type of arras they are and what happens if someone pulls out.

How much is usually paid

There is no percentage set by law. In practice it is often around 10% of the price: enough to make the deal serious without the buyer risking too much before the boat has been checked properly. On low-value boats a round figure is sometimes used, and on expensive boats it may be less than 10%.

Always pay by bank transfer with a clear reference (for example, "arras boat purchase registration…"), never in cash without a receipt.

What the document should say

  • Who the parties are, with ID or tax number. If a company owns the boat, who signs for it.
  • The boat: make, model, registration, identification number (NIB or CIN/HIN), engines and what is included (trailer, tender, electronics, berth if any).
  • The total price, the amount paid as arras and when and how the balance is paid.
  • The type of arras, spelt out in full, and what happens if the buyer pulls out and if the seller pulls out.
  • A deadline for signing the final sale.
  • Any conditions the purchase depends on: a satisfactory survey or sea trial, finance being approved, the berth transfer being authorised… and what happens to the deposit if they are not met.
  • That the boat is delivered free of charges and with its paperwork in order, and who pays each cost.

Deposit and survey: order matters

A common mistake is paying the deposit before the boat has been properly inspected. If the survey then finds a serious problem and the document does not cover it, getting the money back can be difficult.

There are two good ways to do it: carry out the sea trial and survey before paying arras, or pay arras subject to a written condition: "if the survey finds undeclared defects, the buyer may withdraw and recover the deposit". That way both sides know where they stand. The guide on what to check when buying a used boat covers what to look at.

Common mistakes

  • Writing only "deposit" or "reservation" without saying what happens if someone pulls out. Each side then reads it in their own favour.
  • Paying cash or without a document signed by both parties.
  • Paying someone who is not the owner. Check the boat papers to make sure the person signing owns it or has authority to sell.
  • No deadline for the final sale.
  • Forgetting the berth or extras. If they are included, put it in the document.

Frequently asked questions

Do I lose the deposit if I pull out?

It depends on the type. With penitential arras, a buyer who withdraws loses the deposit and a seller who withdraws pays back double. With confirmatory arras there is no right to withdraw: the other party can demand performance or claim damages. That is why the document needs to be clear.

How much deposit is paid when buying a boat in Spain?

There is no legal figure. Around 10% of the price is common, but any amount can be agreed. What matters is paying it by bank transfer with a signed document.

Is the deposit deducted from the price?

Normally yes: the arras count as part of the price and the balance is paid on completion. The document should say so.

Do I need a notary for a boat deposit?

No. A private document signed by both parties is enough, although on large deals some people prefer to have a lawyer review it.
