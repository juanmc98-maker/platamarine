How much does it cost to keep a boat for a year in Catalonia?

By Juan MoranteSeptember 20268 min read

The purchase price is what everyone looks at. What gets forgotten is the rest: mooring, insurance, engine, haul-out, ITB inspection. Here is a realistic breakdown, with market ranges and no optimism, so you can do the maths before you buy, not after.

Mooring: the biggest cost

It is the only cost you pay twelve months a year whether the boat is in the water or not, and the one that varies most with the port and the boat's length: renting a berth in a marina in an expensive area has nothing to do with a concession berth in a quieter port. You have real rates for 19 ports in Catalonia, port by port, in the mooring guide: it is the first figure worth checking before you buy, because it can outweigh everything else put together.

Insurance

Third-party liability insurance is compulsory to go out on the water and, within this budget, one of the cheapest items: for a boat of around 7 metres it is usually about €50-65 a year for liability only. Most owners take out wider cover (own damage, theft, assistance), and then the price goes up to roughly €300-400 a year for that same boat. The bigger and newer the boat, the more expensive the policy.

Engine maintenance

A lot depends on whether it is a petrol outboard or an inboard diesel. An annual service on a mid-range outboard costs around €200-500; a diesel with many hours, including filters and injectors, can reach €500-800 or more. An engine with documented maintenance from the dealer is one of the things most worth asking for before you buy; I explain it in what to check when buying a used boat.

Haul-out, hull cleaning and antifouling

Taking the boat out of the water once or twice a year to clean the hull and renew the antifouling paint is another fixed cost. On a 7 to 9 metre boat, including crane, cleaning and antifouling, it is usually somewhere between €300 and €800 per haul-out, depending on the boatyard and whether anything on the hull needs repairing at the same time. If the boat has been moored for a long time without a haul-out, expect more fouling and a higher cost the first time.

ITB inspection

It only applies from 6 metres on List 7 (and always on List 6); below that there are no periodic inspections. When it is due, it is a few hundred euros every 5 years for an 8 to 12 metre boat, plus the haul-out if you had not planned one. It is explained in detail in the ITB guide.

Fuel

The most variable cost of all: it depends entirely on how much you go out and at what speed. A small boat with an outboard can use something like 20-30 litres an hour at cruising speed; a bigger one with a diesel engine, quite a lot more. If you only go out four or five weekends a year, this item weighs little; if you sail every week in summer, it can be one of the most expensive.

Other costs that are not always counted

  • Sacrificial anodes: replaced every year or two, about €30-80 in parts plus labour.
  • Electronics and batteries: a starter or service battery lasts a few years; replacing it costs €100-300 depending on capacity.
  • Licence, if you do not have one yet: you can practise for free with our PER practice test before signing up with a school.
  • The unexpected: any experienced owner will tell you to leave some margin for whatever was not on the list.

An example, without mooring or fuel

To give you an idea of the fixed maintenance costs (leaving out mooring, which has its own guide, and fuel, which depends on use) for a 7 to 9 metre motorboat in good condition:

ItemApproximate range per year
Insurance (comprehensive)€300-400
Engine maintenance€300-600
Haul-out, cleaning and antifouling€300-800
ITB (spread over 5 years)€50-100
Anodes, battery, small extras€100-200
Total without mooring or fuel€1,050-2,100

Add the mooring at your port (the part that varies most, check it in the mooring guide) and fuel according to how much you go out, and you have the real budget. These are approximate figures: every boat and every port is different, and if you want to fine-tune it for your own situation, it is better to talk it through directly.

How to cut costs without neglecting the boat

  • Choose your mooring carefully: a concession berth usually works out cheaper in the long run than an annual rental, if you are staying for many years.
  • Buy with documented maintenance: a boat that comes from charter or from an owner who kept the invoices brings far fewer surprises than one with no history.
  • Do not skip the small checks: a worn anode or a dirty filter caught in time costs little; the problem they cause if ignored does not.
  • Learn to do the basic cleaning yourself: washing the hull, cleaning the deck and checking levels does not need a technician.

Frequently asked questions

What is the biggest cost of owning a boat?

Almost always the mooring, especially if it is a rented berth in a marina in an expensive area. It is the only cost you pay twelve months a year whether the boat is in the water or not.

Is boat insurance compulsory?

Yes, third-party liability insurance is compulsory to go out on the water. It is one of the cheapest items overall: for a boat of around 7 metres it is usually about €50-65 a year for liability only, although most owners take out wider cover.

Can the cost of keeping a boat be reduced?

Yes: choose a concession berth instead of renting if you are staying for many years, buy a boat with a well-documented engine to avoid surprises, do the basic cleaning yourself and do not skip small checks that cost more if they pile up.
