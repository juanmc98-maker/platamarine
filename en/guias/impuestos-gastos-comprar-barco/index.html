Taxes and costs when buying a boat in Spain: what you pay in each case

By Juan MoranteUpdated October 20265 min read

The listed price is hardly ever what you end up paying. Depending on who you buy the boat from, whether it’s new or used and how long it is, different taxes are added. Here are the cases one by one, so you can do the sums before you agree a price.

Summary: what you pay in each case

You buy…TaxRegistration tax (IEDMT)
Used, from a private sellerITP, depending on your region (5% in Catalonia)No, it’s already registered
Used, from a company or professionalVAT (21%)No, it’s already registered
New, up to 8 mVAT (21%)Exempt (form 06)
New, over 8 mVAT (21%)12% on first registration
New, for charter (list 6)VAT, deductible if there’s genuine business activityCan be exempt (form 05)

Used boat from a private seller: ITP

ITP (transfer tax) is paid by the buyer, and the rate is set by the region where the buyer lives, not where the boat is. It ranges from 1% in Galicia to 8% in some regions for larger boats; in Catalonia it’s 5%, filed on form 620 within one month. It’s calculated on the contract price or on the tax authority’s valuation tables, whichever is higher. The full table is in the ITP guide by region. If you don’t live in Spain, the guide for non-residents covers your case.

Used boat from a company or professional: VAT

If the seller is a company or a professional selling as part of their business, the sale carries VAT instead of ITP. Buying through a broker doesn’t change this: if the owner is a private individual, it’s still ITP.

New boat: VAT and, over 8 metres, the 12%

A new boat pays 21% VAT. If it’s over 8 metres, it also pays the registration tax (IEDMT): 12% on its first registration in Spain, on the whole boat as registered, meaning hull, engines and fitted equipment. That’s why there are so many 7.99 m boats. Up to 8 metres it’s exempt, with a declaration (form 06). Jet skis always pay it. I explain it with examples in the guide to the registration tax.

If it’s for charter: list 6

A boat genuinely used for charter can be registered without IEDMT (with prior recognition from the tax authority, form 05), and the VAT can be deductible if there’s genuine business activity. In return it has to meet the requirements for a commercial boat, and if it stops chartering in the first few years, the tax can come back. I explain it in the guide to list 6 and list 7. Your gestor (tax adviser) needs to look at this based on how you plan to register the activity.

The paperwork

On top of the taxes, there’s the change of ownership at the Harbour Master’s office (Capitanía Marítima), or the registration if the boat is new, which is usually done through a gestoría. Before you pay, always ask for the up-to-date registry sheet (hoja de asiento) to check the boat has no charges against it. The full list of documents is in the paperwork guide.

And what comes afterwards

  • Third-party insurance, compulsory from day one.
  • A berth or, for a small boat, storage and a trailer.
  • The ITB inspection when it’s due.
  • Maintenance: engine, antifouling, safety equipment.

All of this, with numbers, in the guide to what it costs to keep a boat in Catalonia.
