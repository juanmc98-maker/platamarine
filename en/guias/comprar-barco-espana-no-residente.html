Buying a boat in Spain when you are not tax resident

By Juan MoranteUpdated September 20267 min read

More and more people live and pay tax outside Spain (in Andorra, Dubai, Latin America or travelling all year) but spend their summers here and want a boat in the Mediterranean. The questions are usually the same: whose name should it be in, which flag should it fly and which taxes do I pay? This guide explains how it works, so you can go to the agent or tax adviser knowing what to ask.

First things first: are you really non-resident?

Everything else depends on this. The flag and the owner's name come later. For the Spanish tax office you are tax resident in Spain if any of the following applies:

  • You spend more than 183 days in Spain in a calendar year. Occasional absences count as days in Spain unless you can prove tax residence in another country.
  • The main centre of your business or economic interests is in Spain.
  • You are also presumed resident if your spouse (not legally separated) and dependent minor children live in Spain, unless you prove otherwise.

To show that your residence is elsewhere, the usual document is a tax residence certificate issued by the other country's tax authority. Two more points worth knowing before you buy: Spanish nationals who move to a country Spain treats as a non-cooperative jurisdiction remain taxable in Spain in the year of the move and the following four years. And a boat moored all year in a Spanish port, with the berth, insurance and maintenance paid from here, is one of the signs the tax office may take into account when judging where you actually live.

An example

Someone who spends July, August and September in Spain adds up to about 90 days: on days alone, they don't reach 183. But if their partner and children live here all year, the presumption works against them even if they spend most of the year abroad. That's why the first question isn't which flag, but where your residence really is.

Foreign flag and the registration tax

Spain's registration tax (IEDMT) is 12% on boats over 8 metres. It is paid by whoever registers a boat in Spain for the first time, and also by a Spanish resident who uses a foreign-flagged boat here: the law requires them to register it in Spain. If the person using the boat is neither resident nor established in Spain, that obligation doesn't exist, even if the boat spends the whole season here. We cover it in detail in the registration tax guide.

The sensitive point is who uses the boat. If it is in your name but in practice relatives or friends resident in Spain use it while you're away, or if it belongs to a foreign company managed or based in Spain, the obligation can arise. Spanish Customs Surveillance (Vigilancia Aduanera) checks foreign-flagged boats in ports and anchorages every summer for exactly this reason.

VAT and temporary admission

VAT is a separate question. A boat that was bought in the European Union with VAT paid (the norm for a second-hand boat already in Spain) has EU customs status, and that doesn't change by putting it under another flag.

If the boat doesn't have EU VAT paid, for example because it was bought outside the EU, it can stay in Spain under temporary admission without paying VAT or duties, provided that:

  • It is registered outside the European Union in the name of a person established outside the EU.
  • It is used by people established outside the EU, for private use only.
  • It stays for a maximum of 18 months; after that it has to leave the EU customs territory.

If an EU resident uses it, the boat is treated as imported and VAT becomes due at that point. Chartering it to third parties doesn't fit this private-use regime either.

Buying a boat that is already in Spain

This is the most common second-hand case: a Spanish-flagged boat with VAT paid and, if it is over 8 metres, with the registration tax settled when it was first registered. As a non-resident buyer, this is what to expect:

  • NIE. You need a Spanish foreigner's identification number to sign the sale and transfer ownership.
  • Purchase tax. If the seller is a private individual, you pay the regional transfer tax (ITP); in Catalonia it is 5%. If the seller is a company or a professional, it normally carries VAT instead of ITP. The registration tax is not paid again.
  • Flag. You can keep the Spanish flag; the agent will tell you what paperwork the Harbour Master requires. Changing it later is possible, but a new flag doesn't change what has already been paid or your residence position.
  • Licence. On a Spanish-flagged boat you'll need a Spanish licence or a recognised foreign one; on a foreign-flagged boat, whatever the flag state requires. See licences.

So which flag?

Each country has its own registry and requirements: who can register a boat, surveys, which licence it requires and the annual cost. Registries that are unusual for pleasure craft can complicate insurance, entry into some marinas or reselling the boat in Spain; the better-known ones bring fewer surprises.

But a flag doesn't make you non-resident. If you are resident in Spain, the Spanish obligations are the same under any flag, as we explain in the Polish flag and other flags guide.

Frequently asked questions

Can I keep a boat in Spain if I live abroad and only come in summer?

Yes. What matters is that your tax residence really is outside Spain and that the boat is used by you or by other people who are not Spanish residents either. The months you spend here count, but they are not the only thing the tax office looks at.

If I put it under a foreign flag, do I avoid the registration tax?

Only if you are not resident in Spain. For a resident, the flag changes nothing: if they use the boat in Spain, they must register it here and pay the tax if it is over 8 metres.

How long can the boat stay in Spain?

If you are not resident and the boat already has EU VAT paid, the registration tax does not impose a time limit. If the boat is under temporary admission (no EU VAT paid), the maximum is 18 months, after which it has to leave the European Union.

Do I need an NIE to buy a boat in Spain?

Yes. You will be asked for one to sign the sale, settle the relevant tax and transfer ownership at the Harbour Master's office (Capitanía Marítima).
