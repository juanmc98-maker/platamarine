Boating licences

Which licence you need to skipper a boat, and how to get it

By Juan MoranteUpdated September 20267 min read

Many of the people who write to me about buying their first boat don't have a licence yet, or hold the PNB and want a 9-metre boat. Here is the essential rundown of each recreational boating licence in Spain, how the exams work in Catalonia, and what they cost. And at the end, a free PER practice test.

The five licences, from smallest to largest

They are regulated by Royal Decree 875/2014. These are the powerboat privileges; sailing requires the specific sailing practical hours for each licence.

For most of the boats I handle, 7 to 12 metres along the Catalan coast, the licence needed is the PER. With the PNB you're capped at 8 metres and 5 nm, which is enough for many fishing and cruising boats, but it shuts the door on almost anything with a cabin.

The PER, in detail

The PER exam is multiple-choice, 45 questions with four options, 90 minutes, and you pass with a maximum of 13 errors. Three sections also carry their own fail limits: no more than 2 mistakes on buoyage, no more than 5 on collision regulations (COLREGs/RIPA), and no more than 2 on chart work. Blank or wrong answers don't subtract points.

The first six sections are the same as the PNB exam. Since 2014 the PER exam no longer covers radio or engine topics: those are learned during the practical hours.

After the exam come the practical hours: 16 hours of safety and navigation on board, plus the short-range radio operator course (ROCA). With that you can apply for the card. If you also complete 24 hours of offshore passage practice (at least 4 at night), the PER is extended to 24 metres and to crossing to the Balearics. And with 16 hours of sailing practice, it also qualifies you for sailboats.

Exams in Catalonia

In Catalonia the exams are called by the Generalitat, not the Ministry, through the Escola de Capacitació Nauticopesquera. There are nine PNB and PER sittings a year, most of them in Barcelona plus one in Girona and one in Tarragona, and three Yacht Skipper and Yacht Captain sittings, in Barcelona. For the rest of 2026, the published PNB and PER dates are 3 October in Tarragona and 7 November and 12 December in Barcelona; registration closes roughly two and a half weeks before each exam.

You register online through the Generalitat's procedure, paying the fee (around €76 for the PER and €65 for the PNB in 2026). The exam is bilingual, Catalan and Spanish, and for the PER and Yacht Skipper you need to bring the tide table. Once you pass and complete the practical hours, issuing the card costs around €65 more, and you need a medical fitness certificate from a driver-testing centre, valid for two years.

What it costs to get the PER

Looking at the public prices of schools in Barcelona, the Garraf and the Costa Brava, a PER course with theory, the 16 practical hours and the radio course runs €400–500. With sailing practice it rises to €600–900, and week-long offshore courses to the Balearics, which include the extension to 24 metres, run around €1,100–1,350. Add the exam fee, card issuance and the medical certificate (€25–60), so a basic PER usually ends up between €550 and €700 in total.

My advice: check exactly what's included in the price (book, test platform, repeat practical hours if you fail) and which port the school runs its practical hours from, because 16 hours of boat time far from home gets tiring.

Expiry and renewal

The card expires after ten years (five once you're over 70). Renewing it doesn't require an exam: a new medical fitness certificate is enough. You don't lose the licence itself by letting the card expire.

If you're coming from abroad

To sit the exam you only need an ID, foreign resident card (NIE) or passport and to meet the age requirement; you don't need to be Spanish. Licences from European Economic Area countries and from a list of countries in the Royal Decree (the UK, Switzerland, Andorra, Argentina or Canada, among others) can generally be authorised by the Harbour Master's Office to skipper Spanish-flagged boats; requirements change, so it's worth checking there first. Spain doesn't issue the international ICC; anyone who needs it to sail abroad has to apply for it in their country of origin.
