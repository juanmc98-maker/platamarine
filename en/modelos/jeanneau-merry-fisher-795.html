Used Jeanneau Merry Fisher 795: what to check, prices and who it's for

By Juan MorantePrices reviewed September 2026

This is the boat for someone who's out on the water all year: an enclosed helm, a side door for single-handed mooring, a galley, a proper heads and a cabin comfortable enough for a weekend aboard. It's sold hugely well in Catalonia to people who fish in winter and go out with the family in summer. With 150 hp it cruises at 20 knots on modest fuel. It's the best "all-rounder" under 8 metres, in exchange for less sunbathing space and less speed than an open boat.

What to check on a used Merry Fisher 795

Beyond the general points in the guide on what to check when buying a used boat, on this model I focus on this:

  • Window and sliding-door seals: this is where the first water ingress shows up; check for stains on the cabin ceiling.
  • Damp in the bow berth: a boat closed up wet all winter smells; ventilation and a dehumidifier are signs of a careful owner.
  • Engine hours: on this model it's normal to see 400 or 600 hours in five years because it gets used a lot; the service history matters more than the figure.
  • Series 2 (2020 onwards): the wider bow is very noticeable in beam-on conditions; if the budget stretches, it's worth it.
  • Electronics: almost all have a sounder and plotter; check it isn't an old unit with no chart support.

Licence, taxes and fixed costs

Under 8 metres there's no registration tax on a boat already registered in Spain, and skippering it only needs the PNB or PER licence. When you buy it you'll pay transfer tax (ITP, 5% of the price in Catalonia) and the Harbour Master's fee; I detail it in the guide on paperwork and ownership transfer. For mooring, a boat this size costs between €2,500 and €4,000 a year on the Catalan coast depending on the marina, and considerably less in dry storage; rates in the mooring guide.

Alternatives I'd consider

Jeanneau Cap Camarat 7.5 WA (if you'd rather have an open deck); Beneteau Antares 8 (the direct rival, same philosophy). And if you're not sure what type of boat you need, the two-minute test will help.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a used Jeanneau Merry Fisher 795 cost?

It mostly depends on the year, engine and equipment. 2016 to 2017: €45,000 to €72,000 (wide spread depending on engine and kit); 2018 to 2021: €64,000 to €83,000; 2022 onwards (Series 2): €87,000 to €105,000. These are guide ranges taken from listings on European portals in September 2026; the real price is settled by inspecting the boat.

What licence do I need to skipper a Jeanneau Merry Fisher 795?

Under 8 metres, the PNB licence is enough (up to 5 miles from a safe haven), and the PER licence lets you sail up to 12 miles and to the Balearics with the relevant add-on. A Navigation Licence isn't enough: its limit is 6 metres.

Is registration tax due when buying a used Jeanneau Merry Fisher 795?

No, if it's already registered in Spain: the tax is paid only once. Buying it does mean paying transfer tax (ITP, 5% in Catalonia) and the Harbour Master's ownership-change fee.
