What boat should I buy? Take the two-minute test

By Juan MoranteSeptember 2026

Almost everyone who writes to me starts with a brand or model they've seen in an ad. I prefer to start somewhere else: how many of you there are, what you'll do with the boat, where you'll keep it and what licence you hold. From that the type of boat almost picks itself, and then we look for the model. Answer these nine questions and I'll tell you which way I'd go.

The result is a guide based on your answers, not a purchase recommendation: two boats of the same type can be very different. If you'd like, write to me with your result and we'll fine-tune it together.

Frequently asked questions before choosing a boat

What boat should I buy as a first boat?

For a first boat I usually recommend starting with how you'll use it rather than the model: how many of you there are, whether you'll sleep on board, where you'll keep it and what licence you hold. From that the type almost picks itself: an open or a RIB if you want simplicity, a sundeck if what you're after is sun and swimming, a cabin cruiser or a flybridge if you'll spend nights away. The test above sorts it out in two minutes.

What length can I take out with each licence?

Without a licence, up to 5 metres and 15 hp within reach of the coast; with the Navigation Licence, up to 6 metres and 2 miles; with the PNB, up to 8 metres and 5 miles; with the PER, up to 15 metres (24 with the extra practical training). I explain it in the licences section.

How much does it cost to keep an 8-metre boat in Catalonia?

The fixed cost that weighs most is the mooring: around Barcelona most marinas publish rates of €2,700 to €3,700 a year for 8 metres, plus VAT and utilities depending on the marina; publicly-run marinas come out considerably cheaper and Costa Brava marinas more expensive. On top of that comes insurance, the annual haul-out and engine maintenance. You'll find marina-by-marina rates in the mooring guide.

Is an outboard or an inboard engine better?

It depends on size and use. Up to 8 or 9 metres an outboard is simpler and cheaper to maintain and allows dry storage or trailing; beyond that, and on boats with a cabin, an inboard or sterndrive is the norm. The test takes this into account in question 8.
