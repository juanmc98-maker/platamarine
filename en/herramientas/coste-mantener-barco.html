How much does it cost to keep a boat per year?

By Juan MoranteFigures reviewed in September 2026

Enter the length, the engine, how much you will go out and where you would keep it, and you get a yearly budget broken down line by line. These are the same numbers I use when someone asks me before buying their first boat: indicative, but not optimistic.

How each line is worked out

  • Berth: you pick your marina or area and it is worked out from that marina's published yearly rate for 8 and 12 metres, adjusted to your length (VAT added where the rate excludes it). The rates for all 19 marinas, and what each includes, are in the Catalonia berth guide.
  • Insurance: fully comprehensive, around 0.7% of the boat's value with a minimum of €200. Mandatory third-party cover alone is much cheaper.
  • Engine: yearly service by type and power; a high-hours diesel can cost more.
  • Haul-out: once a year with cleaning and antifouling, about €70 per metre. In dry stack or on a trailer you do not need antifouling.
  • ITB: only from 6 metres; the inspection cost is spread over its 5 years.
  • Fuel: average cruising consumption by engine type and power, times your hours. On a sailboat the engine only runs part of the time.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to keep a 7 metre boat per year?

It depends mostly on the marina. With an outboard and about 60 hours at sea a year, it comes to around €6,500 in a Maresme marina and close to €11,000 in the most expensive Costa Brava marinas. Without a berth (dry stack or trailer) it drops a lot.

What is the biggest cost of owning a boat?

Almost always the berth, because you pay it twelve months a year. Then fuel, depending on how much you go out.

How reliable is this calculator?

It gives a realistic order of magnitude to decide before buying. The real cost depends on the marina, the boat's condition and how you use it: for a specific case, it is better to talk it through.
