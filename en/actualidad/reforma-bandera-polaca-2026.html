Polish flag reform: what changes and what to do if you own or plan to buy a Polish-flagged boat

By Juan Morante24 September 20266 min read

For years the Polish flag has been the "cheap and forever" option for many boat owners in Spain. This summer the Polish government took the step everyone expected: a bill to tighten its register. It isn't law yet, but it's worth knowing what it proposes, because it affects both those who already have a Polish-flagged boat and those thinking of buying one.

Where it stands

The Polish Council of Ministers approved the bill on 28 July 2026 and sent it to parliament (the Sejm) the next day, where it is being processed as bill no. 2995. It has been in committee since 11 August. In other words, nothing has changed yet: the bill still has to go through committees, debates and votes, and the final text may change. There is no date of entry into force yet either.

What the bill proposes

According to the summary published by the Polish government itself, these are the main changes:

  • Prior inspection. Boats seeking the Polish flag will first have to be inspected by an authorised organisation, paid for by the owner. Today there is no inspection at all to register a recreational boat.
  • Confirming data every two years. Registered boats will have to confirm or update their data in the register at least every two years. If not, the boat is automatically struck off.
  • No more small boats. Boats under 7.5 metres or with engines of up to 15 kW (about 20 hp) will not be able to obtain the Polish flag.
  • More control. The authorities will be able to strike off boats without valid safety documentation or that fail to confirm their data, and procedures become electronic.

The Polish government's stated aim is to end abuse of the voluntary register, which holds tens of thousands of foreign-owned boats. In Spain we have seen for some time how some agencies register boats with their own contact address; the reform seeks the opposite, so that the authorities can reach the owner.

What doesn't change if you live in Spain

Reform or not, the flag only changes the obligations of the registry country. If you are resident in Spain and use the boat here, you still need the relevant Spanish licence, compulsory insurance and the safety equipment required by Spanish rules, and for boats over 8 metres the Spanish registration tax (IEDMT) can still be claimed. I explain it calmly in the foreign flags guide and the registration tax guide.

If you already own a Polish-flagged boat

For now there is nothing different to do. The sensible thing is to check that the register shows your real contact details, not just those of the agency that handled it, and to keep the registration document safe. If the bill is passed as it stands, you will have to confirm your data every two years; missing that notice could mean the boat is struck off. And if your boat is under 7.5 metres, we'll have to see what the final text says about boats already registered.

If you're going to buy one

Ask for the original registration document and check that the seller appears as the owner. Ask for proof that EU VAT has been paid too (invoice or customs document). And do the numbers: if the boat is over 8 metres and you'll use it in Spain, allow for the registration tax and for the cost of moving it to the Spanish flag if you ever want to sell it here. All of that should be reflected in the price.

If you want to sell it

From what I see day to day, a boat with a foreign flag takes longer to sell and gets negotiated down more: many Spanish buyers deduct what it will cost them to register it here. With the reform up in the air, that doubt will weigh a little more. Before deciding whether it's worth moving it to the Spanish flag to sell, talk to your agent, because it depends a lot on the length and value of the boat.

Sources: summary of the bill published by the Chancellery of the Prime Minister of Poland (gov.pl), information from Marinos.es on the progress of bill 2995, and Spanish Law 38/1992 on Excise Duties.
