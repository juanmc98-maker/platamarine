Why aluminium

Most of the boats you see in the Mediterranean are made of fibreglass. Aluminium is a different way of building, with clear advantages and a few points of care worth knowing before you buy.

What it gives you

  • Lighter for the same strength: with the same engine, more speed and lower fuel consumption.
  • Takes knocks better: if it hits a rock or a pontoon, aluminium dents rather than cracks.
  • No osmosis, the classic problem of older fibreglass hulls.
  • Long-lasting and repairable: it is repaired by welding, and it is recyclable.
  • Allows truly custom builds: it does not rely on moulds, which is why every Brizo can be different.

What needs looking after

  • Galvanic corrosion: you need to keep an eye on the anodes and the shore power connection, and avoid mixing metals without insulation.
  • Copper-free antifouling: standard antifouling contains copper and cannot be used on aluminium. A specific aluminium-safe one is applied over an epoxy primer. The boatyard needs to know this.
  • Insulation: good insulation keeps out noise and heat; on a custom-built boat it is specified from the start.
  • The right yard: in the Mediterranean there are fewer specialist yards than for fibreglass. I can help you find one near your berth.

General guidance only. The specific maintenance for each boat is set out in the shipyard's manual.

How to buy a Brizo

  1. We look at which model suits you: plans, specifications and performance.
  2. We define how you will use it: cabins, cruising area, speed, interior style and any special requests.
  3. The shipyard prepares the proposal: specification, equipment, price and lead time.
  4. We finalise the technical details and the build contract is signed. From there, the shipyard builds the boat, carries out the sea trials and delivers it.
