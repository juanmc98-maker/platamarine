What I check before casting off

By Juan MoranteSeptember 20264 min read

Ten minutes before setting off can prevent many of the rescues you see in summer. Not a manual checklist: it's what I check myself, every time, in this order.

Weather and plan

Wind and sea forecast for the way back, not just the way out. Expected return time, and who you've told. On the Catalan coast in summer, planning the return before the afternoon Garbí sets in avoids many trips back into a head sea.

Engine and fuel

  • Oil and coolant level (on inboards) and no water in the separator filter bowl.
  • Fuel: the thirds rule. One third to go out, one third to come back, one third in reserve.
  • Ventilate the engine compartment before starting if it's petrol (blower for a few minutes, bilge with no smell).
  • Start up and check water is coming out of the cooling telltale (the outboard's "pee stream") and that temperatures stabilise.
  • Batteries: voltage and selector switch in the correct position.

Safety on board

  • Lifejackets in view, one per person, and worn by children at all times.
  • VHF radio on and tested on channel 16 (or your phone charged and with signal if you don't carry a radio in zone 7).
  • Anchor and chain ready to drop without needing to untangle anything, even if you don't plan to anchor: it's your brake if the engine fails.
  • Bailing: automatic bilge pump working and the manual bucket or pump within reach.
  • Flares and fire extinguisher in date, and the first-aid kit somewhere everyone knows.

The boat

Drain plugs in, hatches closed, anything loose on deck stowed, fenders and lines brought in as you leave the marina so nothing falls overboard and ends up in the propeller. A glance at the transom and the swim platform: on many 7–9 metre boats that's where water gets in when there's weight aft.

The crew

How many people are aboard, that it doesn't exceed the plate's rated capacity, and that someone besides you knows how to start the engine, stop it, use the radio and where the lifejackets are. If the skipper falls overboard or gets seasick, that's what matters.

A personal list, not meant to replace the mandatory equipment for your navigation area or the training in your licence course.
