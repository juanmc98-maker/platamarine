Anchoring well in the coves of the Catalan coast

By Juan MoranteSeptember 20265 min read

Anchoring looks easy until the afternoon Garbí sets in around four and half the cove starts dragging. These are the rules I use myself, no fuss: where, how much chain, how to check it's holding, and when to leave.

Where

Look for a sandy bottom; on the chart and the plotter it shows as "A" or "S". Posidonia seagrass (the dark patches) doesn't hold well and is also protected: anchoring on it carries a fine along much of the coast. Check the wind forecast for the afternoon, not the morning: in summer, on the Catalan coast, the Garbí (southwest) typically sets in between two and four, and a cove open to the south becomes uncomfortable. Leave yourself swinging room: the boat will pivot around the anchor with a radius equal to the chain paid out plus the length of the boat.

How much chain

As a reference, three times the depth with chain in good weather, and five or more if there's wind or you're staying overnight. In five metres of depth, measured from the bow (the depth plus how far the stem rises above the water), that's fifteen metres of chain at minimum, and more if you're staying the night with a rising tide. If your chain isn't marked every five or ten metres, mark it: it's the cheapest thing you can do to anchor better.

How

  1. Bow into the wind, boat stopped, anchor ready. Lower the anchor until it touches bottom.
  2. Go astern very slowly while paying out chain, so it lies stretched out and doesn't pile up on the anchor.
  3. With the chain paid out, hold a little astern for a few seconds. If the chain tightens and the boat stops, it's holding. If the chain jerks or the boat keeps moving back, it's dragging: haul up and try again.
  4. Take two landmarks ashore (a house and a rock, for example) or set the plotter's anchor alarm. Check them again in ten minutes.

Beaches and swimmers

Anchoring and navigating are forbidden inside the marked swimming area. On unmarked beaches, within 200 metres of the shore the maximum speed is 3 knots. Even without buoys, if there are people in the water, keep further away than the rule requires: a boat dragging among swimmers is a scare you don't want.

When to leave

If the wind veers and the cove becomes exposed, if the boat next to you drags toward you, or if the bottom hasn't held after two attempts. If the anchoring doesn't feel right, more chain won't fix it; better to move. And when weighing anchor, bring the chain up by hand or with the windlass without dragging the boat: motor up until you're right over the anchor, then haul it up.

General guidance for recreational boats in good weather. With bad weather forecast, the prudent choice is port.
