My take

A 2006 Jeanneau Prestige 32: a 10.65-metre flybridge (manufacturer's figure) with two helm positions, inside and up on the flybridge, which has a bimini.

It has a single Volvo Penta D4 260 hp diesel with around 1,500 hours, according to the owner. It is a very common engine on boats of this size. One engine means a single drive line to maintain; on the other hand, you have less margin when manoeuvring in port than with twins, so it is worth asking whether it has a bow thruster. Real performance and fuel use depend on load, hull condition and propeller: the place to check them is the sea trial.

Below there is a saloon with table, sofa and galley, two cabins (the forward cabin and a second cabin with a television) and a full heads compartment. The photos show well-kept upholstery and woodwork, and the bathing platform with the davits and the tender with its outboard, which is what makes the difference when you anchor in a cove.

I can see it suiting a couple or a family who want weekends and holidays around the Balearics sleeping on board: it has two cabins, a heads and a saloon, plus the flybridge to sit up top. It can be skippered with the Spanish PER or a higher qualification. It is in Menorca and can be viewed by appointment. The price is €89,000; as it is a private sale there is no VAT and the buyer pays the transfer tax (ITP) of their region.

Before reserving, I would ask for the maintenance invoices for the engine and sterndrive (with around 1,500 hours, when the last major service was done), the date of the last haul-out and antifouling, the ITB inspection and its expiry date, the maximum number of people stated in the papers, and whether the berth is included in the deal. And I would do a sea trial and an independent survey.
