Used boat prices in Spain in 2026

By Juan MoranteReviewed in October 2026 · updated monthly

The first thing almost everyone asks, buying or selling, is what a boat is worth. Here are the ranges I work with for the used models that sell most on the Catalan coast, taken from real listings and reviewed every month. They are asking prices, not closing prices: they tell you whether a listing is in line with the market or not.

What pushes the price up or down

  • Year and series. A new series (V2, Series 2) usually means a clear jump in price.
  • Engine and hours. The same length with a more powerful engine or low documented hours can be worth quite a bit more.
  • Service history with invoices. Servicing, antifouling and electronics backed by invoices: buyers pay more for no surprises.
  • Paperwork. Valid ITB inspection, Spanish flag and taxes paid; an imported boat not registered here over 8 metres carries a 12% tax.
  • Berth. A transferable berth in an area with waiting lists adds value.

I explain it in more detail in the guide on how to price a used boat.

How the market looks

According to ANEN, the Spanish boating industry association, 4,992 new recreational boats were registered in Spain in 2025, almost the same as the year before (0.9% down), with a final quarter up 14%. Seven in ten are up to 6 metres long, and the 6 to 8 metre bracket is the one starting to grow. The Balearics and Barcelona register the most boats. Little new supply in mid-size lengths means a well-kept used boat of 6 to 9 metres is still in demand.

Where these numbers come from

Every month I go through the listings for each model on Spanish and European portals, drop duplicates and boats that have not been updated for months, and keep the range where most of the supply sits. It is the seller's asking price: the closing price is usually lower, and how much lower depends on the boat's real condition. For a specific boat, what counts is a valuation with its own details.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a used boat cost in Spain?

It depends on length, type and year. On the Catalan coast, a 6 to 7 metre open boat sells for roughly €20,000 to €60,000, a 7 to 8.5 metre sundeck or walkaround from €25,000 to over €100,000 depending on the year, and an 8 metre pilothouse from €45,000 to €110,000, and one of almost 9 metres from around €95,000.

Is the asking price the real price?

No. It is what the seller asks. The closing price is usually lower, especially if the boat has been listed for a while or is missing paperwork or invoices.
